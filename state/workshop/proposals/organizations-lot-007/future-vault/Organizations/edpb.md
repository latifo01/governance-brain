# European Data Protection Board (EDPB)

## Summary

The European Data Protection Board is a body of the Union with legal personality, established by the GDPR (Articles 68-70). It is composed of the heads of the national supervisory authorities and the European Data Protection Supervisor, acts independently, and is tasked with ensuring the consistent application of the GDPR throughout the EEA.

The corpus documents its active role in AI matters: it issues opinions on the interpretation of the GDPR for AI models (Opinion 28/2024, requested by the Irish supervisory authority under Article 64(2)) and guidelines such as those on the legitimate-interest basis (Guidelines 1/2024). Under the Digital Omnibus proposal (not yet adopted law), it would receive additional roles in supporting and preparing harmonized texts, including DPIA lists and templates.

## Applicability

This note applies when determining the authority of EDPB outputs in AI governance: guidelines and opinions shape supervisory practice and national DPA enforcement, and its consistency mechanism (Articles 64-65) produces binding decisions in cross-border cases.

## Governance Considerations

- Treat EDPB guidelines and opinions as the operational reading of the GDPR for AI systems; monitor them for changes in supervisory expectations.
- Use the consistency mechanism to anticipate how cross-border AI processing will be supervised (lead authority, one-stop-shop).
- Route GDPR-specific obligations for AI through the data protection Knowledge Bank rather than asserting them from this note.

## Limits

The Board's regulatory powers are evidenced through the GDPR text and its own outputs; the Omnibus-assigned roles are proposal-stage only and must not be asserted as current law.

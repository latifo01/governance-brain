# Court of Justice of the EU (CJEU)

## Summary

The Court of Justice of the European Union is the corpus's authoritative judicial interpreter of EU data protection law. Under the GDPR's recitals, its task includes reviewing the legality of EDPB Board decisions in actions for annulment (Article 263 TFEU) and answering preliminary references from national courts on GDPR interpretation (Article 267 TFEU). GDPR application must be foreseeable, with due regard to its case-law.

The corpus documents the CJEU's definitional role through cases the EDPB applies to AI: Meta v Bundeskartellamt (C-252/21) clarified the interpretation of Article 9(2) GDPR and the three-step legitimate-interest test, joining a line of authority (SCHUFA, Breyer, KNLTB) that defines the tests used for AI models and legitimate interest.

## Applicability

This note applies when interpreting GDPR-derived obligations for AI systems: the authoritative reading of lawful basis, special categories and automated decisions comes from CJEU case-law, applied through EDPB guidance.

## Governance Considerations

- Track CJEU preliminary rulings on GDPR articles that govern AI processing (legitimate interest, Article 9, automated decision-making) as binding interpretation updates.
- Treat EDPB guidelines as operationalizations of CJEU tests, not independent law.
- Legal monitoring for AI deployments in the EU should include CJEU dockets on AI Act provisions as the Act matures.

## Limits

This note reports the Court's role as evidenced in the corpus; it is not legal advice and does not predict case outcomes. The AI Act's own judicial architecture is not yet evidenced in the corpus.

## Related Concepts

- [[edpb]] issues guidance that applies CJEU tests.
- [[legitimate-interest]] is defined by Article 6(1)(f) as interpreted by the CJEU.
- [[gdpr]] is the statute the Court interprets.

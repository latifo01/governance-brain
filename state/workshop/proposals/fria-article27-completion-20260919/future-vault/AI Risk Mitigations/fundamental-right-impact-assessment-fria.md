# Fundamental right impact assessment

## Summary

Article 27 requires a deployer-side fundamental-rights impact assessment (FRIA) before deployment for the specified deployers and high-risk systems within its applicability gate. The FRIA is specific to the deployer's intended use and the people and groups affected in that context; it is not an automatic requirement for every AI system or every deployment.

## Applicability

Before deployment, the Article 27(1) obligation applies to a high-risk AI system referred to in Article 6(2), except systems intended for the Annex III point 2 area, when the deployer is:

- a public-law body;
- a private entity providing public services; or
- a deployer of the Annex III point 5(b) or 5(c) systems.

The actor category, system route and stated exclusion must be established before treating the FRIA as applicable. This note does not replace the separate high-risk classification analysis in [[eu-ai-act]].

## Required assessment content

The FRIA records:

- the deployer processes in which the system will be used according to its intended purpose;
- the intended period and frequency of use for each system;
- the categories of natural persons and groups likely to be affected in the specific context;
- the specific risks of harm likely to affect those people or groups, taking account of the provider information supplied under Article 13;
- how the human-oversight measures are implemented in accordance with the instructions for use; and
- measures to take if the risks materialise, including internal governance arrangements and internal complaint mechanisms.

## First use, reuse and updates

The obligation applies to the first use of the high-risk AI system. In similar cases, a deployer may rely on a previously performed FRIA or an existing assessment carried out by the provider. If an Article 27(1) element changes or is no longer up to date during use, the deployer takes the necessary measures to update the information. Reuse therefore requires a similarity check and a currentness check; it is not a blanket substitution for a deployer-specific assessment.

## Notification and questionnaire model

After completing the FRIA, the deployer notifies the market-surveillance authority of the results and submits the completed model referred to in Article 27(5) as part of that notification. Deployers may be exempt from this notification obligation in the case referred to in Article 46(1).

The amended Article 27(5) provides for an AI Office questionnaire template, including an automated tool, and supports relevant cross-references to DPIA sections or the relevant parts of a DPIA. A local questionnaire must not be presented as the AI Office template unless that official template has been separately verified.

## Relationship with DPIA

The amended Article 27(4) allows a deployer, where the Article 27 obligation is already met through a GDPR Article 35 DPIA or the equivalent law-enforcement assessment, to include cross-references to relevant DPIA sections or include relevant parts in the FRIA. This reduces duplicate documentation while preserving the distinct legal purposes of a [[data-protection-impact-assessments-dpia]] and a FRIA.

## Governance considerations

The governance record should identify the deployer decision owner, the classification and role evidence supporting applicability, affected groups, harm scenarios, provider information, implemented [[human-oversight]], internal governance, complaint mechanisms, notification status and update triggers. The Article 113 schedule is route-dependent: the relevant Chapter III rules apply from 2 December 2027 for Article 6(2)/Annex III systems and from 2 August 2028 for Article 6(1)/Annex I systems, subject to the Article 6(5) exception. These dates are not a universal FRIA date for every AI system.

## Limits and currentness

This note is bounded to Article 27(1)–(5), Article 46(1) and the Article 113 schedule covered by the reviewed units. The base Article 27(1)–(3) text is combined with the amended Article 27(4)–(5) text and route-dependent schedule. It does not determine national enforcement procedures, a project-specific classification, or the content of any future AI Office template beyond the reviewed amendment.

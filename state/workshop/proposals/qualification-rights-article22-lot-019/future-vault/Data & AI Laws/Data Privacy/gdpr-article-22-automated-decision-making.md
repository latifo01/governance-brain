# GDPR Article 22 automated decision-making

## Summary

GDPR Article 22 establishes a data-subject right concerning a decision based
solely on automated processing, including profiling, when the decision produces
legal effects or similarly significantly affects the person. The rule must be
assessed against the actual decision process and the effect on the individual,
not only against the presence of an AI component.

## Applicability

Use this note with [[automatic-decision-making-assessment-adma]] when an AI
system or another automated process makes or materially determines an
individual outcome. Record whether the process is solely automated, whether it
uses profiling, the nature of the effect, the responsible controller, and the
legal basis relied upon.

## Operative rule and safeguards

Subject to the exceptions in Article 22(2), the data subject has the right not
to be subject to the solely automated decision described above. The exceptions
are where the decision is necessary for entering into or performing a contract,
authorised by Union or Member State law that includes suitable measures to
safeguard the person’s rights and freedoms and legitimate interests, or based
on the person’s explicit consent.

For the contract and explicit-consent exceptions, the controller must provide
suitable measures to safeguard the person’s rights and freedoms and legitimate
interests. These measures include the ability to obtain human intervention,
express the person’s point of view, and contest the decision.

Article 22(4) restricts decisions based solely on automated processing that
rely on special-category personal data, subject to the conditions and
safeguards specified by that provision.

## Governance considerations

Use the governance record to identify the controller and decision owner, the
actual decision process, the applicable Article 22 exception and the safeguards
available to the person. For the contract and explicit-consent exceptions,
record how the available measures support human intervention, expression of the
person’s point of view and contestation of the decision. For binding corporate
rules, record the Article 22 right together with the associated complaint,
redress and compensation routes. Link the assessment to [[human-oversight]],
[[data-subject-access-requests]] and
[[data-protection-impact-assessments-dpia]] when those subjects are independently
applicable; these links do not establish their applicability.

## Scope boundary

This note does not state the complete operative requirements for a data
protection impact assessment under Article 35. The assigned evidence supports
Article 22 and limited DPIA context only; Article 35 must be assessed from its
own audited evidence.

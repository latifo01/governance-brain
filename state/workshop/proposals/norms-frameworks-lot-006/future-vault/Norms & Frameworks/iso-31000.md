# ISO 31000 risk management

## Summary

ISO 31000:2018 provides guidelines on risk management: principles (Article 4), a framework (Article 5) and a process (Article 6). In the local corpus it is described through ISO/IEC 23894:2023, which names it as a normative reference — its terms and definitions apply — and restates its Article 4 principles with AI-specific implications: integrated, structured and comprehensive, customized, inclusive, dynamic, and based on the best available information.

The framework clauses (5.1-5.4: leadership and commitment, integration, design, implementation, evaluation and improvement) are worked through for the AI context by the same guidance.

## Applicability

This note anchors the Brain's risk vocabulary in the generic risk management canon: any AI risk process built on [[iso-23894]] inherits the ISO 31000 principles and framework. It applies to enterprise risk management generally, not only AI.

## Governance Considerations

- Verify that the AI risk process inherits the six principles rather than inventing parallel ones.
- Leadership commitment and integration (clause 5) are prerequisites: an AI risk process owned only by a technical team fails the framework.
- Use the principle "best available information" to justify documented uncertainty in early-lifecycle AI risk assessments.

## Limits

No standalone ISO 31000 source exists in the corpus; this note relies on its restatement inside ISO/IEC 23894 (a single reviewed source, scope: as described by SRC-0005). The full standard text is not locally evidenced.

## Related Concepts

- [[iso-23894]] is the AI-specific extension whose structure mirrors this guidance.
- [[nist-ai-rmf]] and [[sr-11-7]] are alternative risk taxonomies that can coexist with the ISO 31000 process.

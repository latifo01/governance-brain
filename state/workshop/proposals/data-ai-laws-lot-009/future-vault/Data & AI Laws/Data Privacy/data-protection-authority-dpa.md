# Data Protection Authority (DPA)

## Summary

Under the GDPR, a supervisory authority (data protection authority) is an independent public authority established by a Member State pursuant to Article 51. Each Member State provides for one or more such authorities to monitor GDPR application, with complete independence (Article 52). Cross-border processing is supervised through the lead supervisory authority mechanism — the one-stop-shop (Articles 55-56). Their tasks include handling complaints and investigations, and their powers span investigative, corrective, authorization and advisory instruments (Articles 57-58).

The corpus also documents an evolving interaction with the AI Act: national data protection authorities are noted as well positioned for AI market surveillance and reporting alongside market surveillance authorities.

## Applicability

This note applies to any organization processing personal data in the EU: the DPA is the supervisory counterpart for complaints, breach notifications, DPIA prior-consultation cases and enforcement. For AI systems, the DPA may also act as or alongside the AI Act market surveillance authority, depending on national designation.

## Governance Considerations

- Identify the lead supervisory authority for cross-border AI processing; the one-stop-shop determines the primary regulator.
- Maintain regulator-ready evidence: records of processing, DPIAs, breach logs, and correspondence readiness (72-hour breach notification, Articles 33-34 via [[gdpr]]).
- Track national designations implementing the AI Act: whether the DPA also supervises AI systems changes the reporting and enforcement map.

## Limits

This note reports the GDPR institutional architecture; national DPA powers vary in detail beyond the corpus. It does not assert that any specific DPA has been designated as an AI market surveillance authority.

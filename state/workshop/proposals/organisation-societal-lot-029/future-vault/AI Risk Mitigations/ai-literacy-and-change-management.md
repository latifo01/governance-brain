# AI literacy and change management

## Summary

AI literacy is an operating capability that enables people to understand the
system they use, recognise its limitations and act within their assigned
authority. Change management connects that capability to the lifecycle: when
the system, context, data, risks or roles change, training, operating guidance,
testing and approval should be reconsidered together.

## Applicability

This note applies to people who design, develop, provide, operate, oversee or
are materially affected by an AI system. The required depth depends on the
human-AI configuration, the system purpose, the operating context and the
potential impact of errors. NIST AI RMF material used here is voluntary
framework guidance; it does not establish a universal legal training program.

## Governance considerations

- Define the knowledge, skills and proficiency expected from each role, taking
  account of domain expertise as well as AI-system limitations and risks.
- Provide role-appropriate learning and operating materials covering intended
  use, known limitations, output interpretation, bias or error signals,
  escalation and the conditions under which a person must seek assistance or
  stop relying on an output.
- Involve operators, practitioners and end users in prototyping and testing
  under conditions similar to deployment. Their feedback can reveal whether
  outputs are understandable and whether the operational boundary is realistic.
- Link material changes in data, model, tools, configuration, suppliers,
  purpose, user population or operating environment to a documented change
  review. Reassess risk, validation, human roles and learning needs before the
  changed system is treated as equivalent to the prior one.
- Keep evidence of training access, completion or competence assessment,
  relevant role changes, change decisions, updated guidance and post-change
  monitoring. Training completion alone does not demonstrate safe or lawful
  system use.

## Limits

AI literacy cannot compensate for an unsuitable system, missing oversight,
unresolved data or security risks, or an authority structure that cannot act on
identified problems. Domain expertise and AI expertise are complementary; a
professional user does not need to become a model developer, but does need
enough understanding to recognise when the system exceeds its intended use.
The evidence supports a proportionate governance pattern, not a fixed syllabus
or certification threshold.

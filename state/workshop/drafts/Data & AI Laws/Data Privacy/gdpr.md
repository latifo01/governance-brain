The **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)** (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) is the European Union’s comprehensive legal framework governing privacy, data protection, and the handling of personal data. It applies to any organization—regardless of where it is based—that targets, collects, or processes the personal data of individuals located in the EU/[[eea]].

## 7 Core Principles of Data Protection (Article 5)

Organisations processing personal data must comply with seven fundamental principles:

1. **Lawfulness, Fairness, and Transparency:** Data processing must have a legal basis (e.g., consent, contract, legitimate interest), be fair to individuals, and be transparent via clear privacy notices.
2. **Purpose Limitation:** Data must only be collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes and not used beyond those reasons.
3. **Data Minimization:** Only the minimum amount of personal data necessary for the stated purpose should be collected.
4. **Accuracy:** Personal data must be kept accurate and up to date, with steps taken to erase or rectify incorrect data promptly.
5. **Storage Limitation:** Personal data must be retained no longer than necessary for the intended purpose.
6. **Integrity and Confidentiality (Security):** Organizations must implement appropriate technical and organizational measures (e.g., encryption, access controls) to protect data from unauthorized access, breach, or loss.
7. **Accountability:** The data controller is responsible for complying with all principles and must be able to demonstrate compliance through documentation and records.
## Key Legal Obligations for Organizations

- **Maintain a Legal Basis for Processing:** Ensure every data collection activity relies on one of the six lawful bases (Consent, Contractual Necessity, Legal Obligation, Vital Interests, Public Task, or Legitimate Interests
- **Uphold Data Subject Rights:** Honor requests from individuals exercising their rights, including:
    - **Right of Access** (requesting a copy of their data).
    - **Right to Erasure** ("Right to be forgotten").
    - **Right to Rectification** (correcting inaccurate data).
    - **Right to Data Portability** (transferring data to another provider).
    - **Right to Object / Restrict Processing**.
- **Data Breach Notification:** Report significant personal data breaches to the relevant [[data-protection-authority-dpa|Data Protection Authority]] within 72 hours of becoming aware, and notify affected individuals if there is a high risk to their rights.
- **Privacy by Design & Default:** Integrate data protection controls directly into technology architecture, product design, and default privacy settings from the start.
- **[[data-protection-impact-assessments-dpia]]:** Conduct formal risk assessments before undertaking high-risk data processing activities.
- **[[data-protection-officer-dpo]] & Vendor Contracts:** Appoint a DPO when required (e.g., public bodies or large-scale tracking) and execute binding Article 28 data processing agreements (DPAs) with third-party vendors/processors.
- **International Transfer Safeguards:** Ensure appropriate safeguards (e.g., EU Standard Contractual Clauses or adequacy decisions) before transferring data outside the [[eea]].

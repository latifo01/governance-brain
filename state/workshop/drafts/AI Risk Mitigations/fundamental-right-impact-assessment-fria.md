A **Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (FRIA)** is a structured evaluation mandated under **Article 27 of the EU AI Act**. It requires certain deployers of **high-risk AI systems** to assess, document, and mitigate the potential adverse effects an AI tool may have on individuals' fundamental human rights before putting the system into service.
### Key Highlights of a FRIA

- **Target Audience (Who must perform it):** Mandatory for **public sector bodies** deploying high-risk AI, **private entities providing public services** (e.g., healthcare, education, social housing), and organizations using AI for high-stakes evaluations like credit scoring or life/health insurance pricing.
- **Scope Beyond Data:** Unlike a [[gdpr]] [[data-protection-impact-assessments-dpia]] — which focuses specifically on data handling and privacy — a FRIA evaluates broader societal and human rights, including **non-discrimination, human dignity, freedom of expression, equality, access to justice, and democratic safeguards**.
- **Core Requirements:** The deployer must detail the intended use case, define the period of use, identify specific vulnerable groups likely to be affected, outline potential risks, establish human oversight mechanisms, and file the assessment summary with the EU market surveillance authority.

When conducting a FRIA, deployers must evaluate potential impacts across the relevant titles of the **EU Charter of Fundamental Rights**:
### Core Rights Typically Evaluated in a FRIA

- **Human Dignity (Art. 1):** Risk of reducing individuals to automated risk scores or profiling without human consideration.
- **Non-Discrimination & Equality (Art. 21 & 23):** Risk of algorithmic bias, indirect discrimination, or unequal treatment based on protected attributes (gender, race, religion, disability, age).
- **Privacy & Personal Data Protection (Art. 7 & 8):** Intrusion into private life, surveillance, or unauthorized processing of personal data.
- **Freedom of Expression & Information (Art. 11):** Potential chilling effects on speech, information filtering, or algorithmic censorship.
- **Right to Good Administration (Art. 41):** Entitlement of individuals to receive reasoned, transparent explanations for decisions made or assisted by public sector AI.
- **Access to Justice & Fair Trial (Art. 47):** Ability of affected individuals to understand, challenge, and seek redress against an automated or AI-assisted decision.
- **Rights of the Child (Art. 24):** Special safeguards required when an AI system directly or indirectly impacts minors.
- **Workers' Rights & Fair Working Conditions (Art. 31):** Impact on workplace conditions, intrusive monitoring, or automated performance evaluations.
### Mandatory Structure of a FRIA (Article 27(1))

Rather than focusing on a single rights checklist, Article 27(1) mandates a six-part **assessment process**:
1. **Context:** Detailed description of the deployer's processes and intended purpose.
2. **Timeline:** Duration and frequency of planned AI operation.
3. **Affected Groups:** Identification of specific categories of people and vulnerable groups (e.g., job seekers, patients, children).
4. **Specific Rights Risks:** Mapping specific harm scenarios to affected EU Charter rights.
5. **Human Oversight:** Implementation plan for human intervention and override powers.
6. **Mitigation & Redress:** Specific measures to mitigate identified risks and mechanisms to handle complaints when harm occurs.

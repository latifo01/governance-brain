The **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)** is a voluntary, non-prescriptive framework published by the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology ([[NIST]]). Designed to be sector-agnostic, its primary goal is to equip organizations with structured approaches to manage AI risks, mitigate negative societal impacts, and foster trustworthy AI practices across the entire system lifecycle.
# Core Architecture
The framework is divided into two primary sections: **Trustworthy AI Characteristics** and **4 Core Functions**.
## 1. Characteristics of Trustworthy AI

NIST identifies seven key attributes that define a trustworthy AI system:
- **Valid & Reliable:** Operates accurately within defined limits.
- **Safe:** Does not pose physical, operational, or psychological threats.
- **Secure & Resilient:** Maintains stability against adversarial attacks or environment changes.
- **Accountable & Transparent:** Offers clarity around system decisions and governance.
- **Explainable & Interpretable:** Mechanisms to explain how outputs are derived.
- **Privacy-Enhanced:** Safeguards individual data rights.
- **Fair (with Bias Managed):** Minimizes algorithmic bias and unfair outcomes.
## 2. The 4 Core Functions
The operational backbone of AI RMF relies on four continuous execution blocks:
1. **Govern:** Establishes governance structures, accountability mechanisms, organizational risk tolerance, and culture. _(Cross-cutting function that informs the rest)._
2. **Map:** Analyzes the context, deployment environment, and capabilities of the AI system to identify potential risks.
3. **Measure:** Quantifies, tracks, and evaluates identified risks using qualitative or quantitative metrics.
4. **Manage:** Executes risk mitigation strategies, risk-response allocation, and system maintenance plans.

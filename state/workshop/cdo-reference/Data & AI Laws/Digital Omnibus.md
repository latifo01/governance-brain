The **EU Digital Omnibus** is a major legislative package enacted by the European Union to streamline compliance, eliminate regulatory duplication, and reduce administrative burdens across the EU’s digital, data, and cybersecurity framework. It introduces targeted "quick fix" amendments to existing laws—most notably the **[[GDPR]]** and the **[[EU AI Act|AI Act]]**—to align overlapping requirements and provide practical flexibility for emerging technologies.
### Key Impact on the GDPR

The Digital Omnibus introduces pragmatism to EU privacy rules, addressing long-standing industry bottlenecks while maintaining core protection standards:
- **Relative Definition of Personal Data:** Codifies [[CJEU]] case law by evaluating whether data is "personal" based on the subjective perspective of the specific data controller. If an entity does not hold the means reasonably likely to identify an individual, the data is treated as anonymous for that entity.
- **Explicit Legal Basis for AI Training:** Formally clarifies that controllers can rely on **Legitimate Interest** (Article 6(1)(f)) to process personal data for developing and operating AI models and systems, provided appropriate safeguards are in place.
- **Exceptions for Sensitive / Special Category Data:** Introduces targeted Article 9 exemptions allowing incidental processing of sensitive data for AI model development, operation, and **bias detection/correction**, provided strict safeguards prevent sensitive outputs.
- **Relief on Abusive Access Requests (DSARs):** Grants controllers flexibility to refuse or charge fees for [[Data Subject Access Requests]] that are manifestly unfounded, excessive, or pursued for non-data-protection purposes (e.g., commercial leverage or litigation tactics).
- **Streamlined Breach Notification:** Narrows mandatory [[Data Protection Authority (DPA)]] notifications to cases posing a _high risk_ to individuals (raising the previous "risk" threshold) and extends the standard notification window from **72 hours to 96 hours**.
- **ePrivacy / Cookie Relaxations:** Permits functional tracking technologies without consent when necessary for core transmission, requested service delivery, aggregated usage metrics, or security maintenance.
    

### Key Impact on the EU AI Act

The Omnibus adjusts the timeline and scope of the AI Act to give businesses and market surveillance authorities adequate preparation time:

- **Extended High-Risk Compliance Timelines:** Postpones enforcement dates for high-risk AI requirements to ensure technical standards and regulatory sandboxes are operational first:
    
    - **Stand-alone High-Risk AI (Annex III):** Extended to **2 December 2027**.
        
    - **Embedded / Product Safety AI (Annex I):** Extended to **2 August 2028**.
        
    - **Synthetic Content Watermarking:** Transition period extended to **2 December 2026**.
        
- **New Express AI Prohibitions:** Adds explicit bans under Article 5 against AI tools designed to generate non-consensual sexually explicit content / intimate deepfakes ("nudifier" apps) and child sexual abuse material (CSAM).
    
- **Expanded SME/SMC Relief:** Extends compliance simplifications and lighter administrative burdens previously reserved for SMEs to **Small Mid-Caps (SMCs)**.
    
- **Centralized AI Office Oversight:** Streamlines supervision by granting the EU AI Office primary oversight over general-purpose AI (GPAI) models and systems embedded in Very Large Online Platforms (VLOPs).
    
- **Elimination of Overlapping Requirements:** Clarifies that products covered under sector-specific safety legislation (e.g., Machinery Directive) only need to conform to that sector's safety assessment rather than undergoing double certification under the AI Act.

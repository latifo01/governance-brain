> developments36. 
> 42. Third, SAs should consider whether controllers have assessed the risk of identification by the controller 
> and by different types of ‘ other persons’, including unintended third parties accessing the AI model,  
> also considering whether they can reasonably be considered to be able to gain access or process the 
> data in question.  
> 43. In sum, the EDPB considers that, for an AI model to be considered anonymous, using reasonable 
> means, both (i) the likelihood of direct (including probabilistic) extraction of personal data regarding 
> individuals whose personal data were used to train the model ; as well as (ii) the likelihood of 
> obtaining, intentionally or not, such  personal data from queries , should be insignificant 37 for any 
> data subject . By default, SAs should consider that  AI models are likely to require a thorough 
> evaluation of the likelihood  of identification to reach a conclusion on their possible anonymous 
> nature. This likelihood should be assessed taking into account ‘all the means reasonably likely to be 
> used’ by the controller or another person, and should also consider unintended (re)use or disclosure 
> of the model.  
> 3.2.2 Elements to evaluate the residual likelihood of identification 
> 44. While measures might be taken both at the development and the deploym ent stages  in order to 
> reduce the likelihood of obtaining personal data from an AI model, the evaluation of anonymity of an 
> AI model should also consider direct access to the model.  
> 45. In addition, SAs should evaluate, on a case -by-case basis,  if the measures implemented by the 
> controller to ensure and prove that an AI model is anonymous are appropriate and effective.

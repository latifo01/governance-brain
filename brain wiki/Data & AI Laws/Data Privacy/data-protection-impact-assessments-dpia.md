# Data protection impact assessment

## Summary

A data protection impact assessment (DPIA) is required before processing where a type of processing, particularly when using new technologies and considering its nature, scope, context and purposes, is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. A single assessment may cover similar processing operations presenting similar high risks.

## Applicability

The reviewed GDPR text identifies, in particular, these high-risk situations:

- systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects based on automated processing, including profiling, where decisions produce legal or similarly significant effects;
- large-scale processing of special categories of personal data or data relating to criminal convictions and offences;
- systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area on a large scale.

The list is illustrative of the Article 35(1) high-risk test. It does not replace a processing-specific assessment or establish that every AI deployment requires a DPIA.

## Required assessment content

The DPIA must contain at least:

- a systematic description of the envisaged processing operations and purposes;
- an assessment of necessity and proportionality;
- an assessment of risks to the rights and freedoms of data subjects;
- the measures and safeguards envisaged to address those risks and demonstrate compliance.

Where appropriate, the controller seeks the views of data subjects or their representatives. The assessment is reviewed when necessary and at least when the risk represented by the processing operations changes.

## DPO and prior consultation

Where a data protection officer has been designated, the controller seeks the DPO's advice when carrying out the DPIA. Article 36 prior consultation is a distinct pathway: it concerns cases where the controller must provide the supervisory authority with the DPIA and the information needed for that consultation. It is not a general requirement to consult a supervisory authority for every DPIA. The DPO's Article 39 tasks include advising on the DPIA when requested and monitoring its performance under Article 35; this does not itself determine whether a DPO must be designated.

## AI Act coordination

Where applicable, an AI Act high-risk-system deployer uses the information supplied under Article 13 to comply with its GDPR Article 35 DPIA obligation. The cross-reference does not replace the GDPR applicability analysis. The amended Article 113 schedule makes the relevant Chapter III rules route-dependent: the dates differ for Article 6(2)/Annex III systems and Article 6(1)/Annex I systems, subject to the Article 6(5) exception.

## FRIA coordination

The amended AI Act Article 27(4) allows relevant DPIA sections to be cross-referenced or included in the fundamental-rights impact assessment when the Article 27 obligation is already met through a GDPR Article 35 DPIA or the equivalent law-enforcement assessment. Article 27(5) provides for an AI Office questionnaire template that should support those cross-references where relevant. This coordination reduces duplicate documentation but does not merge the legal tests.

## Governance considerations

Maintain one decision record that identifies the processing, purposes, risks, safeguards, DPO advice, consultation route where applicable and review trigger. Link the record to [[fundamental-right-impact-assessment-fria]] when the AI Act FRIA pathway is separately applicable, and to [[automatic-decision-making-assessment-adma]] when the processing involves a relevant automated-decision scenario.

## Limits and currentness

This note is bounded to the cited GDPR Articles 35, 36 and 39 and the cited AI Act coordination provisions. The currentness audit resolves the cited GDPR text to the official EUR-Lex consolidated version retrieved on 2026-09-19. It does not determine Member State DPIA lists, sector rules, national procedures, future amendments or project-specific applicability.

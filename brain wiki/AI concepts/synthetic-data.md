# Synthetic data for AI governance

## Summary

Synthetic data is data generated by an artificial process rather than directly
observed from the target population or event. In the reviewed local evidence it
is used to extend safety datasets from vulnerability insights and to support
evaluation and red teaming. Its value is therefore tied to a documented
purpose, generation method and validation against the risks and conditions that
matter for the intended use.

Synthetic data can improve coverage of rare or deliberately constructed cases,
but generation alone does not demonstrate representativeness, accuracy,
privacy, fairness or real-world performance.

## Applicability

This concept applies to training, post-training, validation, benchmarking,
red-teaming, simulation and safety evaluation. It is relevant when observed
data is sparse, sensitive, expensive to label, or insufficient to exercise
known failure modes. The same governance questions apply to synthetic prompts,
synthetic conversations, images, audio, labels and other derived datasets.

## Governance Considerations

- Record the purpose, generator, version, source assumptions, generation
  parameters, curation decisions and intended use of each synthetic dataset.
- Keep synthetic records distinguishable from observed outcomes and preserve
  lineage to the source material or vulnerability insight that motivated their
  creation.
- Test whether synthetic cases cover the target risks without introducing
  artefacts, distribution gaps, duplicated patterns or misleading confidence.
- Compare evaluation results on synthetic and appropriate observed or
  independently constructed data; explain where results cannot be generalised.
- Reassess the dataset when the model, threat landscape, modality, policy or
  deployment context changes, and record the decision to retain, revise or
  retire it.

## Limits

The local evidence establishes synthetic data as a documented development and
evaluation practice, not as a guarantee of privacy, representativeness or
model safety. No claim is made that synthetic data is automatically anonymous
or suitable for a regulatory test. Its limitations, validation results and
relationship to observed outcomes must remain visible in the evidence package.
